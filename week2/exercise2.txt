Diffie-Hellman

Setup:
Pick a large prime p and a generator g of the multiplicative group of integers modulo p

x and y should be chosen randomly from {1, ..., p-2}

Alice picks secret x, computes X = g^x mod p, sends X to Bob.
Bob picks secret y, computes Y = g^y mod p, sends Y to Alice.

Alice computes Y^x mod p, Bob computes X^y mod p, and they both land on g^(xy) mod p — the shared secret Z.

If we had a black box that when you feed it g^a, and it spits out a:

Feed X = g^x into the magic box - it gives you back x
Now compute Y^x = (g^y)^x = gˣ·ʸ = Z


OMDL is basically asking: "can you solve k discrete logs when you're one answer short?" — and the assumption is that being one short is enough to keep it hard, no matter how clever you are with your free queries.
